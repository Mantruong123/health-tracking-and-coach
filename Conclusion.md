# 5. Conclusion

This project successfully designed, developed, and evaluated AuraFit, a comprehensive AI-powered health tracking and coaching ecosystem. By leveraging Google ML Kit for on-device pose estimation, FastAPI for backend logic, and Flutter for cross-platform delivery, the system achieves a robust real-time tracking pipeline. On the core exercises tested, the system reached up to 99.3% counting accuracy for Arm Curls and maintained a consistent frame rate of over 20 FPS even on mid-range Android hardware. Four major findings stand out: fusing lightweight edge AI with deterministic state machines provides highly responsive real-time feedback; on-device processing effectively eliminates the latency and privacy concerns associated with cloud-based vision models; side-angle camera placements often yield better 2D spatial tracking than direct front angles due to reduced depth occlusion; and integrating metabolic calculations directly with the tracking logic creates a highly personalized, unified fitness experience.

Several limits should be stated plainly. The tracking accuracy remains highly dependent on environmental factors, particularly lighting and camera angle; the system struggles with self-occlusion, as seen in the lower accuracy (94.6%) for Push-ups; the current exercise library is limited to three foundational movements; the heuristic thresholds used for form correction are strict and occasionally over-detect borderline mistakes; and the personalized diet generation relies on standard metabolic formulas (BMR/TDEE) which cannot account for complex or underlying medical conditions.

Future work follows directly from these limitations: expand the exercise library to include more complex compound movements (such as Lunges, Planks, and Deadlifts); replace the rigid heuristic state machines with a lightweight temporal neural network (e.g., LSTMs) to better understand the contextual flow of a repetition; integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) to provide dynamic, conversational coaching rather than static audio warnings; and collect a broader internal dataset of varied body types and environments to further refine the confidence thresholds.

### 5.1 Limitations
A few limits should be stated plainly. First, the reliance on a single monocular 2D smartphone camera means the system inherently lacks true depth perception. This limitation manifests as visual occlusion, particularly during floor exercises like Push-ups, where the user's torso blocks the camera's view of the off-side limbs. Second, the system's performance is sensitive to environmental variables; poor lighting or loose, baggy clothing can cause the underlying ML Kit model to lose tracking confidence. 

Third, the decision to use rule-based Finite State Machines (FSM) for rep counting and form evaluation, while excellent for minimizing latency, introduces rigidity. The strict geometric angle thresholds occasionally over-detect poor form, penalizing users for borderline acceptable repetitions. Finally, the nutritional and coaching intelligence is based on standardized metabolic formulas (BMI, BMR, TDEE). While mathematically sound, these formulas are generalizations and cannot serve as a substitute for professional medical or nutritional advice for users with specific health conditions.

### 5.2 Future Work
Several next steps follow from these limits: (1) expand the current supported exercise library from the foundational three (Push-ups, Squats, Arm Curls) to a comprehensive suite of bodyweight and dumbbell workouts; (2) transition from rigid heuristic state machines to temporal sequence modeling (such as lightweight LSTMs or Transformers) trained on historical landmark data, which is the likeliest way to intelligently handle occlusion and borderline form; (3) integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) into the backend to allow for conversational, highly personalized AI coaching rather than static text-to-speech triggers; (4) implement a brief 3D calibration phase at the start of a workout to better establish the user's proportions relative to the camera; and (5) conduct extensive beta testing across a wider demographic to refine the system's robustness against different body types and environments.
